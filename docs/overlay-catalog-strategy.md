# Adding county overlays on demand

The counties publish about 25,000 map services and the California State
Geoportal another 6,400 that apply everywhere; this app reads four of them per
county. This document is the plan for offering the rest — a layer catalogue the
user browses per county, and a rendering path that draws whatever they turn
on. [county-gis-inventory.md](county-gis-inventory.md) is the evidence it
rests on.

## What is built

Steps 1 to 5 of the order at the bottom shipped. The control pane has a
**County map layers** card; it opens a catalogue sheet grouped by theme and
searchable, and switching a layer on draws it. `GisPortal` is on
`CountySource`, generated by `tool/gis_inventory/generate_dart.py` into
`gis_portal_registry.g.dart` — 164 portals across 54 counties plus the 13
statewide ones.

Two tiers were added after the original build:

- **A city tier.** `tool/gis_inventory/discover_cities.py` points the
  inventory's ArcGIS Online channels at the 483 incorporated cities and found
  a catalogue for about 130 of them. Each city entry carries its bounding box,
  and `GisMapViewModel.portals` only offers a city's catalogues while the
  viewport is over the city at zoom 10 or closer — so panning into Corona
  makes the City of Corona chip appear, and panning out drops it. Cities
  whose servers were already registered in a county's partner tier (San
  Diego, San Francisco) are deliberately not repeated.
- **Federal roots in the statewide tier.** Census TIGERweb, the USGS National
  Map, the National Weather Service, BLM California, the Forest Service, NPS
  and EPA joined the state agencies in `statewide.py`. Same tier, same
  mechanics; the publisher label is what tells them apart.

### Runtime discovery (national)

The registry above is what an offline inventory found, and that inventory has
been run for one state. Everywhere else a county opened a panel holding only
the eleven federal roots — nothing from the county, nothing from the state,
nothing from the city the map was over. That is the gap
`PortalDiscoveryService` closes.

It asks ArcGIS Online the question the inventory answers offline: *who
publishes map services over this county?* Three channels, because none of them
is reliable alone.

| Channel | Request | Recovers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vanity subdomain | `<key>.maps.arcgis.com/sharing/rest/portals/self` | Cuyahoga County, Maricopa County Enterprise GIS, Utah AGRC — self-verifying, so a wrong guess costs one empty response |
| Name search | `search?q=("<County> County") AND ("<State>")` | publishers whose subdomain is unguessable: the City of Dallas publishes as `DallasGIS` |
| Organisation search | `search?q=orgid:<id>` | the rest of a catalogue, once either channel above has found one item of it |

A fourth was tried and is deliberately absent, twice: `discover_orgs.py`
recorded it and this build re-measured it. **ArcGIS Online's `bbox` parameter
does not restrict results.** A Dallas-shaped box returns global COVID feeds and
Esri basemaps sorted by popularity. Spatial filtering happens after the fetch,
not in the query.

**The geographic gate is what makes the answer safe to attribute.** Search
matches text, and text is ambiguous — thirty-one states have a Washington
County. Every item carries its own advertised extent, so the share of a root's
items that actually fall over this county is measurable at no extra request. A
root needs three items over the county and 60% of its placed items there.
Measured against the counties this was built on, everything real scored above
0.8 and everything spurious below 0.3. A whole-world extent counts as
unmeasured rather than as a claim, because it is the ArcGIS default for an item
nobody set one on — treating it as coverage would pass every publisher on Earth.

Washington County, Utah is the case that shows the gate working: no subdomain
matches, the name matches thirty-one counties, and what comes back is Utah
AGRC, Utah DEM and the City of St. George — because those are the ones whose
items are actually there. An ambiguous county with no local publisher comes back
empty rather than coming back wrong.

**Attribution stays carried, not invented.** A hosted root has its
organisation's id in its own path, so `sharing/rest/portals/<id>` gives the
publisher's real name; anything else falls back to the hostname, which is what
the offline inventory writes down for a self-hosted server too. `PortalTier` is
inferred by a deliberately conservative rule — `countyPortal` only when the name
claims the county, `city` only when it claims a city government, `statewide`
only when it names the state *and* says what kind of body it is, and `partner`
otherwise. `Buckeye, Arizona` is a city, not a state agency; understating a
publisher as a partner is recoverable, misstating one as the county is not.
`PortalOrigin` records which entries were registered and which were found
minutes ago, and the panel says so.

Discovered portals carry a `coverage` rectangle — the union of their items'
extents — and `GisPortal.coversViewport` gates on it, so a publisher drops out
of the panel when the map leaves what it maps. Registry entries carry no
coverage and are unaffected.

### The municipality channel

Gating what was found is not the same as finding what is under the viewport,
and a county-name search is a poor way to reach a city. Two failures, one mild
and one total:

- **Mild.** City publishers ride in on the county's name and then have to
  survive the twelve-portal cap. Dallas County brings in Lewisville and Balch
  Springs; a smaller city in a more populous county would not make the list.
- **Total.** Connecticut abolished county government in 2022. Hartford's
  county equivalent is the *Capitol Planning Region*, which no publisher
  names and no subdomain spells. Searching for it returns two University of
  Connecticut catalogues and nothing else — the same empty panel this whole
  feature exists to fix, one level down.

So `discoverPlaces` asks a second question, keyed on the map rather than on
the county: **which governments are under this viewport, and what do they
publish?** The Census answers the first half live from
`TIGERweb/Places_CouSub_ConCity_SubMCD` — no table is compiled in, because
there are ~35,000 municipalities and only the few on screen ever matter.

Two layers are read, not one, because which one governs depends on the state:
incorporated places (layer 4) across most of the country, and minor civil
divisions (layer 1) in New England and the township states, where the place
layer is nearly empty. **`FUNCSTAT` is what makes reading both safe.** A Texas
census county division and a Connecticut town are drawn on the same layer and
mean opposite things — nobody administers a CCD, so nobody publishes its GIS.
`FUNCSTAT IN ('A','B','C')` keeps the governments and drops the statistical
areas; `C` is in the list because Hartford town, coextensive with Hartford
city, is recorded as consolidated rather than active.

Three measurements shaped the rest:

- **The acceptance gate stays the county's.** Gating on the municipality's own
  box was tried and returns *the same publishers* for Balch Springs and for
  Windsor — the place name is already the discriminator, so shrinking the box
  buys nothing and would need its own calibration.
- **Locality is not a ranking signal.** Share-of-items-inside-the-place scored
  1.00 for almost everything found around Hartford, the Living Atlas included.
  Ranking stays by item count.
- **Tier is still read from the publisher's name, never from which search
  found it.** This is what stops a place-keyed search mislabelling a state
  agency as a city: searching "Windsor" surfaces CT DEEP, and DEEP is tiered
  `statewide` because that is what its own name says.

Esri's own global content channels are excluded by organisation id — the id is
in the root, so it costs no request. Living Atlas and GeoDesign publish
demonstration content over the whole country, pass a geographic test
everywhere, are never anybody's local publisher, and every slot they take is
one a town does not get.

**Bounded twice**, because the map moves constantly and panning across
Hartford County puts six towns on screen at a time: three municipalities per
viewport, ranked by how much of the screen each fills, and twelve per county
for the whole session. The view model adds three cheaper guards before any of
that — below zoom 10 it does not run, it does not run while the last one is
still running, and it does not run when the map has only zoomed further into
ground already searched.

Both searches write into one set keyed by `GisPortal.aliasKey`, so neither
clobbers the other and the panel is capped once, at 24, rather than per
search.

**What it does and does not fix, measured.** The channel closes the *total*
failure and does little for the *mild* one, and the two results should not be
read as equal:

| | County channel | Municipality channel adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capitol Planning Region, CT | 2, both University of Connecticut | +8 — including the state DOT and DEEP |
| Dallas County, TX | 12, including three cities | +2, both unnamed |

Hartford is the win: a county-less region went from two university catalogues
to including two real state agencies. But of its eight additions, four are
unnamed `services*.arcgis.com` rows and two are a university project and a
private company — and neither the Town of Hartford nor West Hartford appears.
Where a county government exists, cities already ride in on the county's name,
so there is little left for this channel to add.

Which points at the next real limitation: **the hostname fallback for a
publisher whose organisation record is private.** Writing `services3.arcgis.com`
in the chip is honest, and it is what the offline inventory writes down too —
but at four of eight rows in Hartford it is the dominant experience rather than
an edge case, and several identical chips are indistinguishable. Fixing it
needs a name from somewhere other than `portals/<id>`; the item `owner`'s
organisation url-key suffix is the obvious candidate and was not pursued here
because a username is not reliably an organisation name. `PortalDiscoveryStatus` keeps meaning *has the county been searched*;
the municipality channel is silent and additive, and the panel's counts come
from `portals`, so they update on their own.

It is lazy, capped and never blocking: about thirty small requests in three
parallel waves, twelve portals per county, cached for the session, skipped
entirely in offline mode, and every failure path returns an empty list. A county
whose search fails still has its registry entries and the national tier.

`flutter test tool/check_discovery.dart` runs it against real counties and
prints what they get.

Four deliberate departures from what follows:

- **The catalogue cache is in memory, not in the database.** A drift table
  meant a schema migration for something that costs one request per catalogue
  per app run. Catalogues are re-read on restart.
- **Vector overlays use a palette, not the layer's `drawingInfo`.** Overlays
  are told apart rather than described. Reading the publisher's own renderer is
  the obvious next improvement and is what the section below argues for.
- **Active overlays are not persisted across sessions.** `reorderOverlay`
  exists on the view model with no UI yet.
- **Partner publishers are shown.** The open question below was answered by
  including them: eight counties have no first-party portal, and their partner
  catalogues are better than an empty panel. `PortalTier` keeps the
  attribution honest — the chip is labelled with the publisher's own name.

Feature overlays are capped at six sub-layers per service and 1,200 features,
because a county layer at county zoom is tens of thousands of polygons.

Everything below is the reasoning the implementation followed, kept as the
design record.

## The one decision everything follows from

**Overlays are a separate subsystem from parcels and addresses. They never
share a model.**

`ArcGisFeatureMapper` exists because Riverside's `CLASS_CODE` is a *known*
field that means the same thing as San Bernardino's `AssessDescription`. That
translation is only possible because someone read both schemas and decided they
matched. An arbitrary county layer — `AIRPORT_INFLUENCE_AREAS`,
`FIRE_RESPONSIBILITY_AREAS`, `Teen311_App_Survey` — has a schema nobody has
read and no equivalent anywhere else.

So an overlay feature is **geometry plus an opaque attribute map**, and the
attribute map is only ever shown to the user as a list of field/value pairs
taken from the layer's own `fields` metadata. Do not widen `Parcel` or
`Address`. Do not generalise `ArcGisFeatureMapper`. The moment an overlay
attribute is parsed into a domain model, the county-neutral guarantee in
[adding-a-county.md](adding-a-county.md) is gone.

The corollary: overlays cannot feed the owner lookup, the situs fallback, or
snapshot download. They draw, they identify on tap, and that is all.

Identify-on-tap is `OverlayFeature.hitTest` plus `_OverlayIdentifyCard`. Three
things about it follow directly from the paragraph above:

- **The card is a field/value list in the server's own order**, because
  nobody has read this layer's schema. Null and blank values are dropped —
  an ArcGIS layer routinely returns forty fields with six populated — but no
  field is renamed or reinterpreted.
- **Overlays are offered the tap before parcels**, newest overlay first. They
  are what the user just switched on, and a parcel is still one tap away
  anywhere an overlay has not drawn.
- **Point features need a tolerance.** A crime incident is a single
  coordinate; without a pixel-sized slack around the tap, converted from the
  current zoom, a dot layer can be drawn but never identified. Polygons use
  containment instead, so a tap anywhere inside a district hits it.

## What the registry holds, and where it lives

Runtime discovery can enumerate a server, and — since
`PortalDiscoveryService` — it can also find *which servers exist*, by asking
ArcGIS Online who publishes over a county. What it cannot do is match a curated
inventory: discovery is a ranked guess gated on a geographic test, while the
registry is what somebody read and verified. So the registry is a seed, not a
fallback. Where it has an entry, that entry wins. San Bernardino needs four roots
(`maps.sbcounty.gov/arcgis`, `/gis`, `/img`, and its hosted org); Los Angeles
needs ten.

`docs/adding-a-county.md` forbids branching on a county id outside
`CountySource`, so the registry belongs on `CountySource`:

```dart
/// Public map catalogues this county publishes, beyond the parcel and
/// address services read directly.
///
/// Empty means no catalogue was found, not that none exists — see
/// docs/county-gis-inventory.md.
final List<GisPortal> portals;
```

```dart
@immutable
final class GisPortal {
  const GisPortal({
    required this.root,          // .../rest/services
    required this.publisher,     // display name for attribution
    required this.tier,          // countyPortal | partner | statewide
  });
}
```

Three properties this shape has that a flatter one would not:

- `tier` is carried, not inferred. A partner layer must never be attributed to
  the county — the inventory keeps CalEMA and UC Davis separate from Colusa and
  Contra Costa for exactly this reason, and throwing that away in the model
  would put it back.
- The 46 first-party counties get their portals from the generated registry;
  the other 12 get an empty list and a statewide-only catalogue. No branching.
- `CountySource.statewide()` can attach the shared statewide tier to every
  county in one place.

`docs/county-gis-inventory.json` is the source; a small generator emits the
Dart. Do not hand-maintain 143 URLs.

The generator must **collapse host aliases**. One server commonly answers on
two names — Riverside on `gis.` and `gis1.countyofriverside.us`, Shasta on
`gis.co.shasta.ca.us` and `gis.shastacounty.gov` — and registering both would
show every layer twice in the panel. The inventory already marks these
`alias`; keep only the canonical root and prefer the `.gov` name.

## Discovery is lazy, cached, and folder-at-a-time

Riverside has 54 folders across 7 roots. Enumerating everything on a county
switch is a hundred-plus requests before the map draws — unacceptable, and
unnecessary, because the user opens the layer panel long after the map.

```
CountySource.portals            (compiled in, free)
  └─ root?f=json                (1 request — folders + root services)
       └─ root/<folder>?f=json  (1 request per folder, on expand)
            └─ root/<svc>/MapServer?f=json   (1 request, on layer toggle)
```

Fetch each level only when the user opens it. Cache each response in the
existing drift database with a TTL — a week is right; county catalogues change
on the order of months, and a stale entry is recoverable by pull-to-refresh.

Prefer the **service** root over per-layer requests: `.../MapServer?f=json`
already carries the `layers` array with ids, names and scale limits. One
request describes a whole service.

## The catalogue is curated, not dumped

31,000 services is not a list a person can use, and a raw dump would put
`HomelessContactForm` next to `Parcels`. The panel needs to rank and filter:

1. **Drop what cannot be drawn.** `GPServer`, `GeometryServer`, `SceneServer`,
   `VersionManagementServer` are not map layers. That is 323 services gone
   before anything is shown.
2. **Group by theme**, using the same keyword classifier the inventory used —
   parcels & assessor, zoning & land use, flood & water, fire & hazard, aerial
   imagery, transportation, boundaries & districts, and so on. The classifier
   lives in one place and is testable against the captured inventory.
3. **Rank within a theme** by whether the service has a description, whether
   its name looks like an internal artefact (`_test`, `DONOTDELETE`, `Form_11`,
   a bare GUID), and whether it advertises `Query`.
4. **Search across everything**, because ranking will bury something someone
   wants. Search is over the cached catalogue, so it is local and instant.

Show the theme groups collapsed with counts. Nobody scrolls 1,600 rows.

## How each service type gets drawn

The inventory measured this rather than assuming it, and the measurements are
what make the matrix non-obvious.

| Type | Path | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FeatureServer layer | viewport query → `PolygonLayer`/`PolylineLayer`/`MarkerLayer` | the common case; 21,252 services |
| MapServer, query-capable | either — vector if the layer is small, `/export` otherwise | Riverside: 104 of 106 |
| MapServer, no query | `/export` image tiles only | San Bernardino: **67 of 132** |
| MapServer, fused cache at 3857 | direct `/tile/{z}/{y}/{x}` | fastest, but rare — 2 of San Bernardino's 24 caches |
| MapServer, fused cache at 2229/6424 | `/export` | State Plane cache; the tile grid does not line up with the map |
| ImageServer | `/exportImage` | already implemented — `ArcGisImageTileProvider` |
| VectorTileServer | out of scope for v1 | flutter_map has no vector tile renderer in this build |

Two pieces of new code, both small:

- **`ArcGisExportTileProvider`** — the `MapServer` twin of the existing
  `ArcGisImageTileProvider`. Same Web Mercator bbox arithmetic; different path
  (`/export` not `/exportImage`) and different parameters
  (`transparent=true`, `format=png32`, `layers=show:<id>`). Factor the bbox
  maths out of the existing provider rather than copying it.
- **`OverlayFeatureSource`** — viewport query returning geometry plus an
  attribute `Map<String, Object?>`. Reuses `ArcGisService`'s envelope building
  and **must** respect `fastPathRecordCount`: a spatially filtered query asking
  for a handful of rows took 42 seconds against the statewide layer where the
  floor took 0.3. That rule is not specific to the statewide layer.

Prefer `/export` when in doubt. It works for every MapServer, needs no schema
knowledge, and renders the county's own cartography — which for a flood zone
or a zoning map is better than anything this app would invent.

## Rendering vector overlays without a symbology guess

A parcel is drawn with the colours in `MapColors` because the app knows what a
parcel is. It does not know what `AIRPORT_INFLUENCE_AREAS` is, so it cannot
pick a meaningful colour.

Two options, and the first is better:

- **Use the layer's `drawingInfo`.** Every ArcGIS layer publishes its own
  renderer — simple, unique-value, or class-breaks — with real colours. Reading
  a simple renderer is a dozen lines and covers most layers; falling back for
  the rest is acceptable.
- **Assign from a palette by layer id**, stable per layer so a layer keeps its
  colour across sessions.

Either way, overlays draw beneath the parcel and selection layers. The user's
selected parcel must never be obscured by something they switched on to give it
context.

## State: which layers are on

`GisMapViewModel` currently holds one `selectedImagery`. Overlays need an
ordered, multi-selection list — order matters because overlays stack.

```dart
List<ActiveOverlay> get activeOverlays;
void toggleOverlay(OverlayLayerId id);
void reorderOverlay(int from, int to);
void setOverlayOpacity(OverlayLayerId id, double value);
```

Persist the active set per county id, so returning to a county restores what
was on. The county id is already an on-disk contract; reuse it, do not invent a
second key.

Each active overlay carries its own load state. One unreachable county server
must degrade to a row that says so, not to a failed map.

## What is deliberately not in v1

- **Offline snapshots.** Redistribution terms differ per layer and the README
  already tracks what each county permits. Adding arbitrary layers to snapshot
  download is a licensing decision, not a code one. Overlays are online-only
  until someone makes that decision per publisher.
- **Editing.** 58 of San Bernardino's services advertise `Create` and
  `Update`. The app never sends them.
- **Vector tile and scene services.** No renderer.
- **Geoprocessing.** `GPServer` tasks are not layers.

## The statewide tier is shared, not per-county

The 13 California State Geoportal roots
([inventory](county-gis-inventory.md#the-statewide-tier-gisdatacagov)) cover
all 58 counties. Copying them onto 58 `CountySource` entries would be 754
duplicated URLs that must stay in step.

They belong in one constant instead:

```dart
/// Statewide catalogues offered in every county, in addition to whatever
/// the county itself publishes.
///
/// These are the same services `californiaCountiesQuery` already reads the
/// county boundary from; this enumerates the rest of them.
static final statewidePortals = <GisPortal>[ ... ];
```

The catalogue service concatenates `county.portals + statewidePortals`, and
`GisPortal.tier` keeps them visually and attributionally separate in the panel
— a statewide group below the county's own, labelled with its publisher
(Caltrans, CAL FIRE, DWR) rather than with the county's name.

Three consequences worth stating:

- **No county opens an empty panel.** Madera, Mono, Solano and Trinity get
  flood zones, fire hazard severity, groundwater basins and state highways
  from day one, and so does every other county.
- **Ranking must be theme-first, not publisher-first.** 2,483 of the 6,446
  statewide services are CDFW habitat and species layers. Sorted by count, a
  user looking for flood zones gets bird ranges. The theme classifier already
  handles this; the point is that the statewide tier makes getting it wrong
  much more visible.
- **The geoportal has a DCAT feed.** `gis.data.ca.gov/api/feed/dcat-us/1.1.json`
  is the entire 3,990-dataset catalogue in one response, with titles, tags and
  publishers the REST endpoints do not carry. It is 21 MB, so it is a build-time
  input to the registry generator, not something the app fetches. Use it to
  enrich the statewide entries with real descriptions; use REST for the rest.

## Worth raising before building

**Whether the 135 county `GeocodeServer`s are in scope.** They are not
overlays, so they do not belong to this feature, but 29 counties publish one
and county geocoding is strictly better than prefix-matching an address table —
which is what `addressSearchField` does today. It is a separate piece of work
that this inventory happens to have found the endpoints for.

**Whether partner publishers are offered at all.** Answered by shipping them:
the eight counties with no first-party portal — SACOG for Placer and Yolo,
CalEMA for Colusa and Plumas, the City of Alameda for Alameda — would
otherwise open a panel holding only statewide layers. Showing
them means a third group in the panel and a third attribution label. Leaving
them out means those counties see only the statewide tier. The registry carries
them either way; only the UI decision is open.

## Suggested order

1. `GisPortal` on `CountySource`, `statewidePortals` beside it, and the
   generator from `county-gis-inventory.json`. Nothing renders; the registry
   exists and is tested.
2. Catalogue service — lazy folder walk, drift-cached, theme classifier.
   Testable against the captured inventory without a network.
3. Layer panel UI, read-only. The user can browse; nothing draws yet.
4. `ArcGisExportTileProvider` and image overlays. This is the path that works
   for every service type, so it makes the feature real fastest.
5. `OverlayFeatureSource` and vector overlays with `drawingInfo` symbology.
6. Per-county persistence and layer reordering.

Steps 1–4 are the feature. 5 and 6 are the polish.
